Pipeline Architecture — 3 Approaches

How the Job Hunter runs in the background. Pick what feels right for you.

A

Continuous 24/7 Pipeline

Campaign runs non-stop once activated. Scrapes new jobs, tailors, applies, monitors email — all the time. No schedule needed.

Pros

  • Fastest to apply — first-mover advantage
  • Never misses a new posting
  • Email replies go out within minutes

Cons

  • Higher scraping cost / proxy usage
  • Needs rate limiting to avoid bans
B

Scheduled Runs (e.g. 3× per day)

Campaign runs on a set schedule — e.g. 7am, 12pm, 6pm. Each run scrapes fresh jobs, applies, checks email. Quiet between runs.

Pros

  • Predictable, controllable
  • Lower resource usage
  • Easy to audit what happened each run

Cons

  • Slower response to new postings
  • Email replies may be delayed hours
C

Continuous Email + Scheduled Apply Recommended

Best of both: job scraping + applying runs on a schedule (e.g. every 6 hours), but email monitoring is always on — interview replies and rejection responses go out within minutes, always.

Pros

  • Fast email response (what matters most)
  • Controlled scraping cadence (avoids bans)
  • Lower proxy cost than full 24/7
  • Clean separation of concerns

Cons

  • Not the absolute fastest to apply

💡 My recommendation is C — email is the most time-sensitive part (an interview invite needs a fast reply), while applying 6 hours late rarely costs you the spot. It also keeps scraping costs low and avoids bot detection from hammering job boards non-stop.
